Overview

Project Overview

Most teams run their customer-facing business across a patchwork of point tools — a CRM here, a project tracker there, invoicing in a third app — with data, permissions, and workflows scattered between them. RocketCRM's vision was bold: one platform where the entire customer lifecycle lives together, multi-tenant from day one, that a team can adopt in minutes yet an enterprise can trust with security, audit, and scale.

Akechi Webcraft designed and built the full RocketCRM experience: six product modules — CRM, projects & work, native billing, workflows & automation, enterprise security, and an AI assistant — architected as independently deployable microservices behind a single API gateway, with tenant isolation, role-based access control, and feature flags baked in. Wrapped around it, a fast, dark-themed marketing website presents the platform with the clarity of a consumer product: transparent pricing, a demo funnel, and a story that turns deep engineering into a simple promise.

Key Features

Our goal was a complete platform, not another point tool — where every module works alone, works together, and is presented so clearly that the buying decision feels obvious.

Complete CRM Suite
01.

CRM, Projects & Billing — Natively Together

The core promise is unification: leads, contacts, companies, and activities with a pipeline that converts and a timeline you can trust; project planning with work breakdown, Kanban boards, and time logging; and billing built in — plans, subscriptions, invoices, and payments handled natively with usage visible at a glance. No integrations to babysit, no data silos.

Microservices Architecture
02.

Microservices Under the Hood, Simplicity on Top

Every module is its own independently deployable service — 20+ in total — behind a single API gateway answering in under 100 milliseconds. Multi-tenant from day one, each workspace gets full data isolation while sharing infrastructure engineered for a 99.9% uptime target. Modules can be scaled, updated, or toggled per tenant without touching the rest of the platform.

Enterprise Security

A SOC2-ready architecture with role-based access control down to the individual action, SSO/SAML for enterprise identity, and a full audit trail on every change. Feature flags govern what each tenant can see and do, so compliance teams get the guarantees they need while everyday users never feel the weight of it.

Workflows & AI Assistant

Automation & an AI Assistant That Does the Busywork

Modules are wired together with events, so the right thing happens at the right time automatically — a won deal can open a project, a completed milestone can trigger an invoice. On top sits an AI assistant that answers questions, searches across everything, and drafts the busywork, turning the platform from a system of record into a system of action.

Project Challenges and Their Solutions

Building a platform that must feel simple to a five-person startup and safe to an enterprise brought real engineering tension. Here is how we resolved it.

1. Replacing Five Tools Without Building a Monolith

Challenges

Covering CRM, projects, billing, support, and automation in one product risks the classic all-in-one trap: a tangled monolith where every feature slows down every other, and where one module's failure takes the whole platform down.

Solutions

We architected the platform as 20+ independent microservices behind a single API gateway — each module owns its data and deploys on its own schedule, communicating through events. Users experience one seamless product; engineering gets isolation, independent scaling, and a 99.9% uptime target with sub-100ms gateway latency.

2. Multi-Tenancy That Enterprises Will Trust

Challenges

A shared SaaS platform must guarantee that no tenant can ever see another's data, that access can be controlled to the level of a single action, and that every change is accountable — or enterprise security reviews will end the deal before pricing is discussed.

Solutions

We built tenant isolation, RBAC, and audit into the foundation, not as an afterthought: a SOC2-ready architecture with role-based permissions on every action, SSO/SAML integration for enterprise identity, and a complete audit trail on every change. Per-tenant feature flags let each workspace enable exactly the modules it pays for.

3. Making Deep Engineering Feel Simple to Buy

Challenges

The platform's strongest selling points — microservices, multi-tenancy, RBAC, event-driven automation — are precisely the terms that glaze the eyes of the operations manager or founder actually choosing a CRM.

Solutions

The marketing site leads with outcomes, not architecture: "One platform for the whole customer lifecycle" and six plain-language module cards, with the engineering story framed as a single reassuring line — "microservices under the hood, simplicity on top." Hard numbers (20+, 99.9%, <100ms, SOC2) appear as trust badges, letting technical evaluators verify depth without burdening everyone else.

4. A Conversion Funnel for Two Very Different Buyers

Challenges

A five-person startup wants to swipe a card and start today; an enterprise wants SSO, SLAs, a dedicated CSM, and a conversation with sales. One funnel built for either buyer alienates the other.

Solutions

We designed a dual-track conversion journey: self-serve tiers at $29 and $99 per month with a 14-day free trial, no credit card, and cancel-anytime reassurance for fast movers — and a parallel enterprise track with custom-scale pricing, "Get a demo" CTAs throughout, and a talk-to-sales path for teams that buy through procurement.
