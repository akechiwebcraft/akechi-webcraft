Overview

Project Overview

Selling an ERP is hard: the product is powerful, technical, and deeply industry-specific, while the buyer — a marble trader in Rajasthan, a timber merchant in Madhya Pradesh, a mandi operator in Uttar Pradesh — wants a simple answer to one question: "Will this work for my business?" Bahi Khata Pro needed a commercial website that could explain six ERP modules and six industry solutions without overwhelming anyone, speak to customers in their own language, and convert interest into free-trial signups.

Akechi Webcraft designed and built the Bahi Khata Pro website as a fast, modern single-page application with full internationalization across 11 Indian languages, light and dark themes, a four-tier pricing system with monthly/yearly billing toggle, customer testimonials, a multi-platform download hub, and a layered conversion toolkit — sticky offer bars, exit-intent popups, and trust-signal CTAs at every scroll depth. The result is a marketing site that sells enterprise software with consumer-grade clarity.

Project Challenges and Their Solutions

Marketing enterprise software to small-business India brings unique friction points. Here is how we solved Bahi Khata Pro's.

1. Explaining a Complex ERP Without Losing the Buyer

Challenges

Bahi Khata Pro spans purchases, lot-based sales, cutting, inventory, ledger, payments, and multi-tenant management. Presenting all of it risked a wall of jargon that would scare away the exact small-business owners the product serves.

Solutions

We applied a benefit-first information architecture: one anchor promise ("Khatabook simplicity, Tally power"), six plain-language module cards, and progressive disclosure — headlines carry the value, detail lives one click deeper. A walkthrough video section shows the product working instead of describing it.

2. One Website, Six Very Different Industries

Challenges

A marble trader cares about slab wastage; a steel trader about heat numbers; an agri-trader about mandi rates and farmer payments. Generic ERP messaging would resonate with none of them — but six separate websites was not an option.

Solutions

We designed an industry solutions grid where each of the six verticals gets its own card written in that trade's vocabulary — cutting optimization, seasoning tracking, BOM management, mill test certificates, POS integration, commission tracking. Matching testimonials from a marble owner, wood director, steel MD, and mandi manager close the credibility loop for each vertical.

3. Reaching Bharat in Its Own Languages

Challenges

The target customer often prefers Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, or Bengali over English. Most SaaS websites in India ship English-only, silently excluding the majority of the addressable market — but maintaining 11 language versions by hand is unsustainable.

Solutions

We built a structured internationalization system — every string on the site lives in per-language translation files loaded on demand, with URL-based locale routing (/en, /hi, /ta…) for shareable, SEO-friendly language pages and a persistent language switcher. Adding or editing a language is a content task, not a development project.

4. SPA Speed and SEO for a Marketing Site

Challenges

A rich, app-like single-page experience with themes, carousels, and popups can easily mean heavy JavaScript, slow first paint on mobile networks, and weak search-engine visibility — fatal flaws for a site whose job is acquiring organic traffic.

Solutions

We engineered for performance and discoverability together: vendor code-splitting so React, UI, and i18n libraries load in parallel cacheable chunks, font preconnects, a pre-paint theme script that eliminates dark-mode flash, and a per-page SEO head component managing titles, descriptions, Open Graph, and Twitter cards — all served from a global edge CDN.
